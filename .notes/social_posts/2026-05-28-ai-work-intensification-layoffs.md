There's a paradox in 2026 that doesn't fit a clean headline.

Three Harvard Business Review studies all found the same thing: AI doesn't reduce work — it intensifies it. Task scope expands. Boundaries blur. Workers report a new kind of cognitive overload they're calling "brain fry."

And yet May 2026 saw 28,000+ tech layoffs. Year-to-date is past 113,000. 48-61% explicitly cite AI.

These aren't contradictions. They're the same structural shift: the "human offset." Fewer workers doing more, harder work.

The defining feature of this wave? Companies cutting while reporting record revenues. Meta cut 8,000 at a $56B profit quarter. Cloudflare cut 20% at 34% revenue growth. Intuit laid off 17% while raising guidance — same day.

The money funding AI infrastructure comes from payroll. Not necessarily the same jobs — the budget lines.

What's emerging is a new taxonomy: Builders and sellers survive. Measurers (finance, compliance, middle management) get absorbed by AI. Coordinators consolidate. AI-adjacent specialists explode in demand. Entry-level hiring collapses — creating a pipeline crisis 5-10 years out.

The real takeaway: domain expertise becomes your moat. The deeper and more distant from what AI can replicate, the harder you are to replace.
