CoinEx has announced an orderly wind-down after nine years. New registrations have stopped, futures are reduce-only, spot trading ends on 29 September, and withdrawals remain available until 22 December 2026.

After BitMEX, BitMart, and AscendEX, it is tempting to blame one thing. The reality is less tidy. CoinEx cites a prolonged market downturn, lower trading volume and liquidity, rising regulation, and compliance costs that no longer made commercial sense.

Did funding rates dry up?

Not directly. Funding is paid between long and short perpetual traders. It is not the exchange's revenue. Near-zero funding can signal balanced positioning or low conviction, but the business pain is lower spot and derivatives volume, thinner open interest, less fee revenue, and fixed costs for market making, security, custody, compliance, and support.

Traditional-finance competition matters, but it is not a complete explanation. ETFs, brokers, and banks now offer simple crypto exposure inside accounts investors already use. SGX is also set to launch Bitcoin and Ether perpetual futures for institutional, accredited, and expert investors on 24 November. Meanwhile, the largest crypto-native venues retain the deepest liquidity and biggest regulatory budgets. Mid-tier exchanges are squeezed from both sides.

The numbers I would watch: net deposits, spot volume, perpetual open interest, order-book depth, and compliance cost per active customer. Funding rate alone is a weak proxy.

For users, the rule is simpler: close positions, reconcile balances, and withdraw well before the final deadline.
